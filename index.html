      Failure walkthrough

      The link that only failed hot.

      A composite example of the faults this line exists to catch. There are no program specifics here, just how I work a problem from first symptom to a test that stops it from coming back.

      1. 1

        Symptom

        Pretest is clean, but a few hours into run-in a handful of trays start logging PCIe correctable errors. After a reset, one slot comes back at half width. A quick re-test at idle passes, which is exactly what makes this kind of fault dangerous.

      2. 2

        Hypotheses

        Three candidates: a marginal riser, cable or connector; thermal, with link margin collapsing once the tray heat-soaks under load; or BIOS link-equalization settings that don't suit this board revision.

      3. 3

        Isolation

        Rule out configuration first: check firmware and BIOS against the manifest. Then a Python/Bash loop logs link width and speed, error counters and BMC temperature telemetry every cycle, so the moment errors start can be lined up against the temperatures. Finally, swap the riser between a failing and a passing slot to see whether the fault follows the part or the position.

      4. 4

        Root cause

        The fault follows the riser, and only above a certain temperature. That points to a riser batch with too little signal margin, which passes cold and degrades once the tray is hot.

      5. 5

        Fix

        Quarantine the batch and replace the risers on the affected racks. File the corrective action with logs and reproduction steps attached, then re-run run-in on the repaired racks and confirm zero new errors.

      6. 6

        Prevention

        Pretest now checks that every PCIe device trained at its expected width and speed, and run-in samples error counters each cycle with a fail threshold. A degrading link now fails run-in instead of slipping through to Fabric, or to a customer.
